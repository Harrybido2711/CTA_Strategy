## 1. Why a market recap belongs in a CTA course

- A CTA is not an equity strategy. The book runs **equities, commodities, FX and rates at once**
  ([01 · What Is a CTA](../docs/01-what-is-cta.md)) — index ETFs, energy, metals, Treasuries, the
  dollar, and inside equities the sector sleeves: tech, energy, utilities, miners.
- So one week contains **several different stories**, one per sleeve, driven by different things on
  different clocks. Energy answers to geopolitics and shipping; rates answer to one committee;
  equities that week answered to positioning and leverage.
- Reading only the equity tape means being surprised by two thirds of your own book — and worse,
  attributing a loss to the signal when it belonged to a sleeve you were not watching.

| Sleeve | What moved it that week | Kind of driver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Commodities** | crude bid hard, gold sold off | supply routes — a physical constraint |
| **Rates / bonds** | volatility up, direction unresolved | one policy committee losing its consensus |
| **Equities (AI)** | a violent drop, then a violent snap-back | leverage unwinding, not news |

## 2. Commodities — crude bid, gold offered

### 2.1 The pairing is the surprising part

Crude rose hard and gold fell hard over the same stretch. The reflex reading — *conflict, therefore
gold up* — was wrong that week, and the lecture attributes both legs to the same set of conflicts
without spelling out the gold leg.

### 2.2 Why crude: it is a supply story, not a demand story

- **Russia–Ukraine has not gone quiet.** It had simply stopped leading the news. Ukraine's sea
  outlets were closed off: one destroyed outright, and the Black Sea route effectively shut because
  merchant ships entering it were being struck, so the traffic stopped.
- **Strikes on Russian oil infrastructure.** Ukrainian drone attacks on refining and export
  facilities — described in class as carried out at US direction, per statements from both sides.
- **And then on Iranian oil shipping**, in the days immediately before the lecture.
- **The Strait of Hormuz.** Roughly a third of the world's oil transport passes through or near it.
  Stack that against the above and a large share of *seaborne* crude was disrupted at once.

The mechanism to hold on to: **the barrels exist and cannot move.** Price rises because the route is
constrained, not because anyone wants more oil. That is why the move persists rather than
mean-reverting the way a demand scare does — and it is also why it feeds straight into the inflation
argument in § 3.

*Added.* The gold leg is left unexplained in the lecture, and the natural candidate is in the next
section rather than this one: gold pays no coupon, so its price is heavily a **real-yield** trade.
A hawkish repricing of the policy path lifts real yields and pressures gold — which would make the
same week's rates story the driver of both. Stated as the likely link, not as something established.

## 3. Rates — the committee lost its consensus

### 3.1 What changed

| | Previous meeting | This meeting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vote** | 12–0 to hold | **9 to hold, 3 for a hike** |
| **What it says** | one view | the unanimity is gone |

Two more things sat on top of the vote:

- **The chair reads dovish but played hawkish** in the press conference. The next day's *Wall Street
  Journal* read it as the market's expectation of future hikes having risen materially.
- **The data went the other way.** The most recent US economic prints were weak — which is the
  argument for cutting, not hiking.

### 3.2 The story is the volatility, not the direction

- The two inputs point opposite ways: hawkish rhetoric and a split committee against a weakening
  economy, with § 2's crude bid feeding the inflation side of the argument.
- So the market cannot settle on a direction, and what rises instead is the **dispersion of views**.
  Bond volatility went up without a trend to go with it.
- That is a **regime statement, not a directional one** — precisely the kind of thing a trend signal
  handles badly and a regime label is for. And it is the rates sleeve, so the instrument is **MOVE**
  rather than VIX ([04 § 3.4](../docs/04-volatility-regimes.md)).

**One aside worth keeping.** On the Wednesday a macro head went out publicly and aggressively calling
for a hike. It did not happen, and the motive was not obvious to anyone in class. Filed as an
illustration that a loud public call is positioning or persuasion, not information.

## 4. Equities — the AI unwind

Covered in full in [Leverage, Deleveraging, and the Time-Horizon Lesson](leverage-deleveraging-and-horizons.md);
the short version, for the cross-sleeve picture:

- A heavily levered AI fund blew up, was liquidated in a block, and the names it held snapped back
  violently the next session.
- **Nothing in it was news about the companies.** The drop and the rebound were both **positioning**
  — levered holders being forced out, then the discount being marked back up.
- Which is why it belongs in a recap of drivers: an equity sleeve can have a violent week with no
  fundamental input at all, and a signal that reads price alone cannot tell the two cases apart.

---

## 5. What to take from it

- **The sleeves run on different clocks.** A physical supply constraint (§ 2) persists for as long as
  the route stays closed; a policy split (§ 3) resolves on a meeting date; a deleveraging (§ 4) is
  over in days. A single lookback cannot be right for all three.
- **A quiet week in equities is not a quiet week.** Rates were the volatile sleeve here while the
  equity index went nowhere on net.
- **This is the argument for a per-sleeve regime label** rather than one number for the whole book —
  the point [04 § 3.4](../docs/04-volatility-regimes.md) makes about VIX and MOVE, arriving here from
  the market rather than from the statistics.

## Key terms (EN ↔ ZH)

| English | 中文 | Meaning here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| supply shock | 供给冲击 | price rises because the goods cannot reach the buyer, not because demand grew |
| Strait of Hormuz | 霍尔木兹海峡 | the chokepoint near which roughly a third of oil transport passes |
| hold / hike / cut | 持平 / 加息 / 降息 | the three things a policy committee can do to the rate |
| hawkish / dovish | 鹰派 / 鸽派 | leaning toward tighter policy / toward easier policy |
| vote split | 票型分歧 | the committee no longer voting unanimously — itself a signal |
| real yield | 实际利率 | nominal yield less expected inflation; what a non-yielding asset competes against |
| MOVE | 债券波动率指数 | the implied-volatility index for the Treasury market, as VIX is for equities |
| positioning | 持仓结构 | who owns the asset and how levered they are — a driver independent of fundamentals |
