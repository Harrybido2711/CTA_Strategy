A CTA book runs equities, commodities and rates at once, so one week holds three different stories.

| Sleeve | What happened | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Commodity** | crude up, gold down | Russia–Ukraine is not over — Ukraine's sea outlets destroyed or closed, merchant ships in the Black Sea struck. Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil infrastructure, said in class to be at US direction, and lately on Iranian oil shipping. Roughly a third of the world's oil transport passes near the **Strait of Hormuz**. Supply constrained → crude bid |
| **Bond** | volatility up | The Fed went from **12–0 to hold** to **9 hold / 3 for a hike**. The chair reads dovish but played hawkish. At the same time US data came in weak, so the market has no consensus on hike or cut. On the Wednesday a macro head publicly and aggressively called for a hike; it did not happen |
| **Equity / AI** | a levered AI fund blew up | Concentrated long the AI complex — SanDisk, data-centre and compute names, Nvidia, Microsoft — short **Adobe** as the hedge, at **4× leverage**. The market went the other way → blow-up → liquidated as a discounted **block trade**. The Thursday after, those names ripped, Microsoft **+10% in a session** |

**Was it a hunt?** Two readings of the same event, and the split is about horizon rather than skill:

- **Quant.** Nobody needed to arrange it. Small funds copied the public holdings with a lag, entered
  near the high, levered up, and ran first on the initial dip — that cascade is the deleveraging
  behind the crash. If a fund that big has blown up, the levered holders are out, so this is a low —
  which is why quants were buying that day.
- **Fundamental.** A large share of the AI holder base *was* those levered funds. Even if they come
  back they come back smaller, so reclaiming the old high is hard.
- **The horizon is the difference.** Quants look a week to a couple of months out, rarely past a
  quarter, and read regime shifts off machine signals so they can leave quickly. Fundamental
  investors have far fewer observations and move later and more conservatively. Neither is wrong —
  "never short America" holds on a twenty-year lens and says nothing about the next month.
